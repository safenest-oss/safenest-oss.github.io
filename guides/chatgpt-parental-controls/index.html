ChatGPT parental controls: what to set up, and what they cannot do

ChatGPT lets a parent link their account to a teen's account and manage selected features. These safeguards are useful, but they are not access to the teen's chats or a full activity monitor.

The key point: linking accounts does not let a parent read conversations, view chat history or monitor real-time activity.

Before you start: age requirements

  • Under 13: ChatGPT is not intended for independent use by children under 13. In educational settings involving children under 13, an adult must conduct the actual interaction with ChatGPT.
  • Ages 13–17: OpenAI requires parental consent before use. Link accounts where appropriate and agree on clear family rules.
  • 18 and over: teen parental controls are not the right tool for managing an adult's account.

1. Link the parent and teen accounts

  1. On the parent's ChatGPT account, open Settings → Parental controls.
  2. Select Add family member, then invite by email or phone number.
  3. The teen opens the invitation and accepts it while signed in to their own ChatGPT account.
  4. Return to Settings → Parental controls and select the teen under Family members.

Open parental control settings

2. Settings worth reviewing

Reduce sensitive content

Keep this on. It adds safeguards around graphic content, viral challenges, sexual or violent roleplay and extreme beauty ideals. It cannot guarantee every answer will be appropriate.

Quiet hours

Schedule sleep, school or family offline time. During these periods the teen cannot use ChatGPT, including ChatGPT Work. This is the setting that restricts access at specific times.

Study mode and Study hours

Eligible new conversations can start in Study mode. Study hours do not block access to ChatGPT and do not apply to GPT or Project conversations.

Additional features

Review Voice, image generation, Health, saved memories, Cloud browser and Codex network access separately. Turn off features the teen does not need.

Conversation privacy

Consider turning off “Improve the model for everyone” if you do not want conversations used to improve models. This is a data-use choice, not a content filter.

Availability and defaults depend on account, region and rollout. Some changes may take time or apply only to new conversations.

Important differences

  • Study hours are not a block. Eligible new conversations start in Study mode, but ChatGPT remains accessible.
  • Quiet hours do block access. The teen cannot use ChatGPT during scheduled quiet hours.
  • Codex and Cloud browser are ChatGPT Work controls. Turning off Codex network access does not turn off regular ChatGPT search, apps or plugins. Cloud browser is a separate control.
  • Turning off Health removes Health and its connected data, but does not prevent general health questions in ordinary chat.

3. Add device-level controls

  • iPhone and iPad: use Apple's Screen Time, ideally configured through Family Sharing. Set Downtime, app limits and Content & Privacy Restrictions. Keep the Screen Time passcode with the parent.
  • Android: consider Google Family Link, app limits and device schedules.
  • Computer: use the operating system's family controls (such as Microsoft Family Safety or Apple Screen Time) and a separate child user account.

Menu names and capabilities vary by OS version, country and family setup. Device limits work separately from ChatGPT parental controls.

4. What parents can and cannot see

Parents can

  • manage selected teen settings;
  • schedule Study hours and Quiet hours;
  • receive limited safety notifications in certain serious situations.

Parents cannot

  • read chats or chat history;
  • watch real-time activity;
  • get a full account usage report.

Either person can unlink the accounts. If a teen unlinks, the parent is notified and can no longer manage settings or receive safety notifications for that account. This is why conversation matters as much as technical controls.

    Family rules to discuss together

    • ChatGPT can help with learning, but it does not replace your own thinking or checking sources.
    • Do not enter passwords, addresses, phone numbers, identity documents or private information about other people.
    • If an answer feels worrying or concerns health, safety or relationships, talk to a trusted adult.
    • Do not use AI to bully, impersonate, deceive or create harmful content.
    • Agree when ChatGPT is useful and when it is time to put the device away.
